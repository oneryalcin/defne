# Pilot UX Research Frame

## Purpose

Define how to evaluate the first version of the app as a family pilot for one parent and one child.

The pilot is not a public product launch. UX research should focus on whether the app creates effective learning time and improves vocabulary retention.

## Primary Research Question

Does the app help the child learn, remember, spell, and use difficult vocabulary words better than simple flashcards or parent-led review?

## Secondary Research Questions

- Does the child willingly complete short practice missions?
- Does the child understand the difference between meaning, usage, and spelling practice?
- Do hints help without making the task feel like the answer is being given away?
- Does the visual character/story layer support memory rather than distract from learning?
- Can the parent identify which words need attention next?
- Does the app create focused learning engagement rather than social-media-style compulsion?

## What To Measure In The Pilot

Learning signals:

- Same-day recall.
- Next-day recall.
- Recall after several days.
- Spelling error reduction for known traps.
- Ability to choose the word in a new sentence.
- Ability to type or explain the word without multiple-choice support.

Engagement signals:

- Whether the child starts a session willingly.
- Whether the child completes a 10 to 15 minute mission.
- Where the child asks for hints.
- Whether the child wants another short round for learning reasons.
- Whether the child can stop without distress or pressure.

Parent signals:

- Whether the parent can add real difficult words.
- Whether the parent can understand the progress summary.
- Whether the parent knows what to practise next.
- Whether the parent trusts the app's diagnosis of weak words.

## What Not To Optimise Yet

Do not spend pilot effort on:

- Public onboarding polish.
- General accessibility compliance beyond obvious blockers for the pilot users.
- Multi-parent workflows.
- Classroom or tutor mode.
- Payment or subscription.
- Marketing pages.
- Full self-serve content operations.

These become relevant only after the pilot shows strong learning value.

## Heuristic Focus For The Pilot

Use a narrower UX bar:

- H1 Visibility of system status: child knows progress; parent knows what is sticking.
- H2 Match with real world: vocabulary explanations make sense to a Year 5 child.
- H5 Error prevention: app avoids reinforcing wrong spellings or wrong meanings.
- H6 Recognition over recall: child can use hints and context before production tasks.
- H9 Error recovery: wrong answers produce useful next practice, not shame or confusion.

Accessibility should still be checked for obvious blockers for the actual pilot users, but full WCAG hardening is a later productisation step.

## Pilot Findings Format

Use this format after real sessions:

```text
Observation:
Evidence:
Learning impact:
Engagement impact:
Confidence:
Next investigation:
```

Separate observed facts from interpretation.

Example:

```text
Observation:
She answered "reluctant" correctly in multiple choice but misspelled it as "reluctent" twice.

Evidence:
Two attempts in one session; same spelling error repeated after a hint.

Learning impact:
Meaning may be partially learned, spelling is not stable.

Engagement impact:
No visible frustration; she accepted the spelling clue.

Confidence:
Medium.

Next investigation:
Check whether she can spell it correctly tomorrow without seeing the word first.
```

## Pilot Success Criteria

The pilot is promising if:

- The child completes several short sessions without parent pressure.
- At least some red/orange words move towards stable recall over days.
- Spelling traps reduce for repeated words.
- The child can recognise practised words in fresh sentences.
- The parent can identify what changed after each session.
- The app creates focused learning time without becoming a reward-chasing distraction.

The pilot is not promising if:

- The child only plays for visuals/rewards and avoids the hard recall work.
- Words improve only inside one session but fail after a delay.
- The parent cannot tell what the app is doing.
- Hints routinely reveal answers too early.
- Spelling remains weak while words appear mastered.
